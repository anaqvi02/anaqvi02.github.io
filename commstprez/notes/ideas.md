# How the plan changed

## The audience

8:30 am, half-asleep first years. So: talk less, have them talk to each other more. Every presenter turn is short and ends in them discussing. Still teach, but don't go on and on.

## Versions of the flow

1. **First draft.** Teach the eight responses, then let them try it. Felt like a lecture with a quiz stuck on the end.
2. **Try it first.** One activity up front, then the clip, then the names, then discussion. Better, but the jumps between parts felt sudden.
3. **Smoother bridges.** Added lines that carry each part into the next and split who runs which part.
4. **Secret cards.** Everyone gets a card telling them how to reply. Dropped: handing cards out in an 8:30 class is a mess, and everyone has already been in this situation anyway.
5. **Reviewer pass.** Four reviewers (audience, presenters, a TA checking the rubric, a teaching expert) found it ran 17–21 minutes, taught the eight types twice, taught the lesson during the activity (against the rubric) and pushed people to share personal stuff. Cut it to 14:45 and made sharing optional.
6. **The paper flow (final, Ali's idea).** Start with Marvin's text. Everyone writes their real reply on paper, reads it out, then talks about why they sent it. Only then show a spread of replies (some pretty bad), sort them onto the continuum, quote, clip. Go back to their own paper. End with a new friend, Johnathan Zheng, who bombed a midterm. His text doesn't ask for a plan, to see whether replies start on the listening side this time.

## Why the paper

Everyone has a real reply in front of them before any terms come up, so the lesson lands on something they wrote, not a made-up example. The only thing they need is a scrap of paper.

## Slide rules we settled on

- A slide shows only what the audience needs: the text message, the question they're discussing, the quote, or a term with its plain meaning.
- No instructions to the presenters on the slides, and no chopped-up fragments.
- Typing animations go one step per click so we can talk between them.
- Say "discuss", not "talk to your row".
- No dots or polls during the takeup. Just ask them to think about where their own answer landed.
- A waiting slide up front while the room settles.

## Looks we tried

Swiss grid and a flat pastel version both got dropped. Final look: glossy clay shapes, cobalt and gold, League Gothic headings.

## Still open

- The friend in the activity is named Marvin, same as a presenter. Could rename him (for example, Sam).
- Merged "share" and "why" into one 3-minute slide, and cut the separate takeup slide (it repeated Round 1).
- The activity runs about 3:50; stretching writing to 60 s and "why" to 2:00 gets it into 4–5 minutes.
