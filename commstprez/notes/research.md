# Research notes

Everything here is from Adler et al., *Interplay*, 6th Canadian ed., Chapter 5 (cited as "Adler") (printed pp. 139–169). Page numbers were checked against the book.

## The main idea: the listening response continuum (Fig. 5.2, pp. 153–154)

Eight ways to respond when someone brings you a problem, from just listening to fixing it.

| # | Response | Plain meaning | Side |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Silent listening | Staying quiet and paying attention | Listening |
| 2 | Questioning | Asking to understand more | Listening |
| 3 | Paraphrasing | Saying it back in your own words | Listening |
| 4 | Empathizing | Showing you get how they feel | Listening |
| 5 | Supporting | Reassuring or standing with them | Fixing |
| 6 | Analyzing | Explaining what's really going on | Fixing |
| 7 | Evaluating | Judging it | Fixing |
| 8 | Advising | Telling them what to do | Fixing |

The book calls the first four more reflective and the last four more directive.
