# Slide guide: Listening

One-line cues for what each slide does and the main thing to say. Use with `script.md`.

1. **Waiting — “Listening. We’ll start in a moment.”** Leave this up while people settle and get paper. Click once to begin.
2. **Marvin texts — “What do you send back?”** Read Marvin’s message. Ask everyone to write their actual reply. The 45-second timer is automatic.
3. **Share what you wrote. Why did you send that?** Three minutes to discuss: read replies out, then talk about what they were trying to do for Marvin. Listen for examples to bring back.
4. **What people send Marvin.** Show the eight reply bubbles together. Ask which sound like what the room wrote; note how varied the instincts are.
5. **Eight responses.** Sort the same replies onto the continuum, from listening to fixing. Click to reveal each type and photo. Keep the mini-lesson brisk; name each and give its plain meaning.
6. **Quote.** Read the p. 164 line: advising is the most common response. This connects the room’s replies to the chapter.
7. **The clip.** Play *It’s Not About the Nail*. No narration over the clip.
8. **Where he landed.** Place his responses on the continuum. Point out the empathizing moment and her “thank you.”
9. **Round 1.** Bring them back to what they wrote: where does their reply land, and is that what they intended? Ask what they wanted when they were Marvin.
10. **Now, think about where your own answer landed.** Hear a few replies. Paraphrase first, then suggest where each response fits. Invite correction.
11. **Was he wrong?** Quick show of hands. The point is not that advice is always bad.
12. **Before giving advice.** Reveal the three checks, one per click: is it needed, is it wanted, have you listened first?
13. **Priya texts — “What do you send her?”** New friend, explicitly asking for help. Ask everyone to write a reply (30 seconds).
14. **Discuss. Is it different from your Marvin reply?** Give them 45 seconds to compare and read a couple aloud.
15. **Where does it land?** Place a couple of replies on the continuum. The point: advice can fit when it’s asked for, and listening can still come first.
16. **Thanks.** Close with “Listen first. Fix if they ask.” Thank the class and hand off.
