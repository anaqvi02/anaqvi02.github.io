# Submission

**Presenters:** Ali Naqvi and Marvin
**Chapter:** *Interplay*, 6th Canadian ed., Chapter 5: Listening
**Concept:** the listening response continuum (Fig. 5.2, pp. 153–154)
**Quote:** "When we're approached with someone's problem, the most common reaction is advising." (p. 164)

## How we chose the topic

We chose advising because it's the response almost everyone reaches for without noticing. The class starts by writing the text they'd send a stressed friend, then discussing why they sent it, all before any terms come up. We then show a spread of real-sounding replies, sort them onto the listening response continuum, and show the clip *It's Not About the Nail* (Jason Headley, 2013). The discussion goes back to the reply each person wrote. The session ends with a new scenario where the friend does ask for advice, so the class applies the book's checks for when advice helps.

## Activity (about 4 minutes, before any discussion)

1. A friend, Marvin, texts: "ugh. headache all week, barely sleeping, two midterms thursday and i work like every night." Everyone writes the reply they would actually send.
2. Students share what they wrote with the people next to them, without explaining.
3. They discuss why they sent it: what were they trying to do for Marvin?

## Final questions

1. Where does your reply to Marvin land on the listening response continuum? Is that what you were going for? (Fig. 5.2, pp. 153–154; mindless listening, p. 143)
2. Has someone ever fixed your problem when you just wanted to vent? What did you actually want? (advising, p. 164; "I know exactly how you feel… let me tell you about it," p. 162)
3. Was he wrong? When is advice the right call? (advice needed, wanted, in the right sequence, pp. 164–165)
4. New scenario: a friend bombed a midterm and asks "what do i do". What do you send, and did it change from your first reply? (choosing a response, pp. 166–167)
5. *(Backup)* "I guess men are just like that" (p. 159). Is the clip funny because it's true, or because it's a stereotype?
